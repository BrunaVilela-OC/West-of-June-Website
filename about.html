Hi, I’m Bruna

I’m a Type A Creative. Yes, that’s a thing. Let me explain.

I’ve always had both a creative brain and a systems brain. I love ambitious ideas and good storytelling, and I also love structure, and figuring out how to make a creative vision actually happen without flattening the thing that made it exciting in the first place.

This isn’t a personality I developed when I became a producer. It’s always been part of who I am. I studied film production and acting, minored in business, and even started an MBA, which probably tells you most of what you need to know.

Here’s my story:

I started as an actor in my teen years, so storytelling has been my passion for a long time. I trained at a conservatory in NYC. The highlight of my time there was one day when my teacher very bluntly told me I’d never make it, that I didn’t have what it takes. I locked myself in the bathroom and, unfortunately, believed that teacher for a very long time.

However, as a person grounded in faith, I knew I couldn’t let this teacher stand between me and what I knew God was calling me to do in storytelling. So after some time, I doubled down. I didn’t just study acting and on-screen performance; I studied production and took every gritty, low-level job that kept me close to the craft. I was a PA. I was an unpaid intern more times than I’d care to admit. I even became a certified master electrician, wiring up stage lights that nobody would ever notice unless they went dark. There was zero glamour in it, but it was the best education in storytelling I could have asked for. Lights can also tell amazing stories in their own way.

Eventually, that all somehow made me a Producer. I started in traditional media, then moved into long-form digital content. I spent four years running weekly shows for a production network, learning exactly what it takes to hold a show, and its people, together. These days, I’m especially drawn to original, story-driven content and the people ambitious enough to bring it to life. Whether it’s for a media company, a creator, or something that hasn’t been built yet, that’s the kind of work I want to be part of.

Along the way, I made myself a promise: I will never, ever be the person who makes someone feel small in a room I’m running.

Story is what I care about. Structure is what lets it grow into something bigger.
That’s the kind of producer I am.

Here’s what all of that adds up to:

I’ve been the actor crying in the bathroom stall, questioning everything. I’ve been the crew member nobody bothers to look at twice unless something goes wrong. And now, I’m the producer keeping the entire engine running in the background.

All of these roles have shaped how I lead a room, how I talk to creators and talent, and how I show up for my team and my crews. It’s hard to describe, but it’s a difference you feel the second we start working together.

Working in traditional media taught me the value of a well-run production. Clear roles, good communication, and systems that keep things moving. I’ve carried those lessons into digital media, where the pace is different, but the need for thoughtful production is just as real.

At the end of the day, I am simply obsessed with stories. A well-told story can quietly shift how someone sees themselves, their neighbor, or the world around them.

That’s the producer I try to be, in whatever room I’m in.

How I work
People over product
One of my favorite quotes: “the happiest bees make the tastiest honey.” The project matters, but the people making it matter more.
Honest communication
Production can be a roller coaster. I’ll be honest about when we hit a peak and when we’re going through a valley.
Not just a yes person
I believe good collaboration means bringing ideas to the table, asking thoughtful questions, and being willing to challenge an approach when I see a better way forward. The goal isn’t to win an argument. It’s to make the work better.
Faith grounds everything
My faith is where all of this comes from. It is the reason I treat people the way I do, tell the truth when it is inconvenient, and keep my word after the excitement wears off.
